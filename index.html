📖 Grown-up’s preparation guide

A topic-based path for turning an approved word list into steady progress.

1. Start with the organizer’s current list

Ask the school or competition coordinator which list, age division, round rules, and year apply. For Scripps, check the official Words of the Champions resources and current rules. Local bees may differ. The words in this game are original examples, not an official syllabus.

2. Organize words by skill

Use four helpful lenses: sound and spelling patterns; syllables and endings; prefixes, roots, and suffixes; meaning and context. Keep the organizer’s levels visible. A word may fit more than one garden. Not every bee tests all language arts topics.

3. Teach each word in a small cycle
  1. Hear and say the word.
  2. Explain its meaning and use it in a sentence.
  3. Clap syllables and notice the spelling pattern or meaningful word parts.
  4. Cover it, spell from memory, then check and identify one tricky spot.

Only teach an origin or word-part explanation when it has been checked in a reliable dictionary. The game avoids guessing at etymology.

4. Review what the child is ready to retrieve

Mix a few familiar words with a few new ones. Bring missed words back after a short break, then the next day, later in the week, and the following week. Let the child tag a word “tricky” to keep it in the review garden. Praise noticing, retrieval, and calm recovery.

5. Borrow practice habits from past winners

Champion and coach interviews have described organizing study, learning word structure and origins, asking useful questions, and reviewing missed words. Those are study ideas, not promises about future rounds. A past champion preparation interview is available from TIME.

6. Rehearse the competition routine kindly

Practise listening carefully, breathing, repeating the word, and asking for a definition, sentence, or origin only when the actual rules allow it. Keep mock rounds short and celebrate persistence over perfect scores.
