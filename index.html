Source and edition

This page uses the 4,000 study-word entries transcribed from the user-provided 2019–2020 *Words of the Champions* PDF. The booklet identifies Merriam-Webster Unabridged as the Bee’s official dictionary. It is an older edition; its introductory tier count for Three Bee conflicts with the count of printed entries. The app follows the actual section labels and study words, and marks the edition as archival. Confirm your child’s current list with their school or organizer.

Read current Scripps study-resource information at spellingbee.com/study-list and the official Words of the Champions FAQ.

Teach a complete word, one at a time
  1. Say, listen, and repeat the word.
  2. Look up a reliable definition and part of speech.
  3. Use the word in a clear sentence; discuss nearby context clues.
  4. Break the word into spoken syllables. Note sound-letter patterns.
  5. Explore roots, prefixes, suffixes, or language of origin only after checking a reliable dictionary.
  6. Cover the spelling and retrieve it from memory. Record the tricky part and schedule a return.
Use a humane review rhythm

Start with a manageable handful of new words, practise them in short rounds, and let confidence guide the pace. Missed words return in the review queue. Take breaks. Do not confuse “seen” with “learned”; look for accurate recall on more than one day.

Prepare for the actual bee

Rehearse a calm routine—listen, pause, repeat the word, then ask for a definition, sentence, or origin only when allowed. This site’s mock bee is a practice activity, not an official contest simulator. Competition syllabi and rules vary by organizer.

How to use a newer list

Choose My added words in the Word Library and paste the word list supplied by the current organizer. For richer study cards, use the dictionary link on each word to add a checked meaning, sentence, and spelling clue. Family notes and progress remain in this browser.
